Prompt-strength trend: positive means the varied side tends to win more as its prompt moves from Level 1 to Level 4; negative means it tends to win less; near 0 means no consistent direction.
Prompt-strength trend formula: let w_s(l) be varied-side win rate for case/varied-side series s at Level l. For each series, T_s = [(w_s(2)-w_s(1)) + (w_s(3)-w_s(1)) + (w_s(4)-w_s(1)) + (w_s(3)-w_s(2)) + (w_s(4)-w_s(2)) + (w_s(4)-w_s(3))] / 6. The displayed metric averages T_s across all ten series.
Prompt-strength trend (ignoring Level 1) applies the same pairwise-gap calculation to Levels 2, 3, and 4.
Level 3 to 4: D = average_s[w_s(4) - w_s(3)].
Fixed-history instability averages p(1-p) across saved histories, where p is one policy's win rate over ten judgments of that history.
Total score: S = T + D - I, using unrounded Prompt-strength trend T, Level 3 to 4 D, and instability I.
